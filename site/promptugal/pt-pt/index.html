PrompTugal on Mistral: European or Brazilian Portuguese

European or Brazilian Portuguese, on Mistral

PrompTugal writes for Portugal, so its Portuguese has to be European, not Brazilian. We gave five Mistral models the same brief, a rain notice for Porto from PrompTugal's data, and asked each for European Portuguese and, as a control, for Brazilian Portuguese, three times each. Code then scans every text for the words where the two usually differ: autocarro or ônibus, comboio or trem, telemóvel or celular, "está a chover" or "está chovendo". Our own runs, with n and the date: not a benchmark.

What we found

30 calls to api.mistral.ai on 21 November 2026 between 02:07 and 02:09 UTC, 3 per model and variant, at the API's default temperature, with the brief below. mistral-large-4's six were then made again, with a larger token budget, between 02:10 and 02:13 UTC; those are the ones shown.

  • Asked for European Portuguese, ministral-3b-2512, ministral-8b-2512, ministral-14b-2512 and mistral-small-2603 wrote European Portuguese in every run: 12 of 12 texts, with no Brazilian marker in any of them.
  • The control shows the scan can tell the two apart: asked for Brazilian Portuguese, the three Ministral models wrote Brazilian Portuguese in 8 of 9 runs. The ninth (ministral-8b-2512, run 3) mixed them: "ônibus", "trem", "celular" and "está chovendo", then "utilizadores", "registar-se", "equipas" and "estão a trabalhar".
  • mistral-small-2603, asked for Brazilian Portuguese, wrote European Portuguese all three times ("autocarros", "comboios", "telemóvel", "está a chover"). Good for PrompTugal; worth knowing if you want Brazilian Portuguese from it.
  • mistral-large-4 reasons before it answers, and the reasoning counts against max_tokens. At 500 tokens it wrote nothing; at 4,000 it wrote nothing in 5 of 6 calls, spending all 4,000 on reasoning. The one text it wrote, asked for European Portuguese, was European. So it is in the table, with too few texts to say more.
  • Every figure in every text written (hours and dates such as 06h00, 18:00 and 21 de novembro de 2026) was found in the brief's facts.

What happens to a key from a QR code

Opened as a link: the browser never sends the part after "#" to any server, and this page removes it from the address bar and this tab's history the moment it loads. Your camera app or browser may still keep the link in its own history, so treat that link like the key itself, and revoke the key in your Mistral console after the event. As a picture: decoded with your browser's own QR reader or, where it has none, jsQR, a copy kept on this site. Either way the page asks you first whether to use it, because anyone can send a link with their own key in it; then the key goes into the key box above, held in this tab's memory only, and is never shown.

Where your key goes

It goes in the Authorization header, and only when you press a button that sends something to Mistral. It is never stored, never shown, never sent to this site or to PrompTugal, and gone when you close or reload the tab. Use a key with a spending limit.

The brief, the list, and what it does not claim

The brief is written in English, so it hands the model neither variant's words, and differs between the two variants only in "European Portuguese, as written in Portugal" or "Brazilian Portuguese, as written in Brazil". Its facts are from PrompTugal's IPMA warnings file (Porto, orange, rain, 06:00 to 18:00 on 21 November 2026, read by PrompTugal at 18:15 UTC on 20 November, as read on 21 November 2026). It is in variant-brief.js.
