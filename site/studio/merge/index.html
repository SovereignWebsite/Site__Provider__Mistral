The studio, flagship

From a shoebox to a website

Drop in what a small business actually has: a flyer or a PDF, a few photos, a voice memo from the owner. Six Mistral models read, look, listen, plan, draw and design, and you get a website you can download and own. Every call goes from your browser to Mistral with your own key, and you see each one before it is sent.

A real run on a fictional bakery, replayed from a file on this site. Nothing is sent.

1. What you have

Up to one document, four photos and one voice memo. They stay in this tab until you send them, and then go only to Mistral.

Your document, photos and recording are sent to Mistral (api.mistral.ai or the EU endpoint you chose) to be read. Use only files you have the right to share: no other people's personal details, faces or voices without their agreement, and nothing confidential. Try the samples instead. Location data in photos is removed before sending: each photo is redrawn in this tab first.

        2. Your Mistral key

        Your key stays in this tab's memory and goes only to the Mistral endpoint you choose.

        If your browser offers to save this key as a password, choose Never.

        No key set.

        Mistral's Free mode: what you send may be used to train Mistral's models unless you switch that off, so send nothing confidential.

        Where your key goes

        It goes in the Authorization header, and only when a call is sent. It is never stored, never shown, never sent to this site, and gone when you close or reload the tab. Use a key with a spending limit.

        On Mistral's free plan, what you send through the API may be used to train Mistral's models unless you switch that off in your Mistral admin panel (how to opt out, as read on 21 November 2026).

          How it works, and what it does not claim

          • The six calls, in order: POST /v1/ocr (mistral-ocr-latest), POST /v1/chat/completions once per photo (mistral-small-2603), POST /v1/audio/transcriptions (voxtral-mini-transcribe-3), POST /v1/chat/completions (mistral-medium-latest, Mistral Medium 3.5, with a JSON schema), POST /v1/conversations with the image generation tool (mistral-medium-latest), and POST /v1/chat/completions (codestral-2508). All go to the Mistral endpoint you chose, and nowhere else.
          • The words on the site are written by Mistral Medium 3.5, but each fact and paragraph must carry quotes from your document or memo, and is checked in code; the model writes no HTML. The page is built in code from the checked plan, so nothing the model writes can run.
          • The stylesheet is written by Codestral. It is kept only if it loads nothing from anywhere (no @import, no outside url()). A small fixed stylesheet, guard.css, loads after it to keep the name, the menu, the captions and the footer readable.
          • The header picture is drawn by Mistral's image tool and served from Mistral's storage at mistralaiblackforestprod.blob.core.windows.net, which this page shows but cannot copy (it does not allow pages to read it). The zip therefore carries a geometric header in your photos' colours, drawn in your browser; save Mistral's picture yourself if you want it. Its link stops working after about an hour.
          • The photo captions and alt text are written by Mistral Small from your photos; check them, as a model can see what is not there.
          • Under Mistral's Commercial Terms of Service the customer "owns all Output" (section 3.1), as read on 21 November 2026.
          • The recorded run used the fictional sample: a flyer drawn in code, three photos made by Mistral's image tool and a memo spoken by Voxtral. Forno da Ribeira is not a real business. The run is in recorded.json, as the models answered.
