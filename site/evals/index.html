Evals of Mistral models for a sovereign website

A sovereign website gives its model six small jobs: write a short About paragraph, pull facts out of a page as JSON, use tools under an Agent Behaviour Policy, ignore instructions hidden in a page it reads, translate into Portuguese and French while keeping product names, and refuse to make up a price. These are the results of running each job on several Mistral models, with every check, token and millisecond published.

Read this first

  • Small numbers. Each suite has 3 or 4 cases, each run 3 times per model, so a pass rate is out of 9 or 12. A difference of one or two passes means little.
  • One run, one day, one place: the date and time are below, the calls went from one cloud container through one network proxy, to Mistral's global endpoint (https://api.mistral.ai/v1), which makes no promise about where inference runs. The EU endpoint was not used for these runs; you can choose it on the run page. Latency includes that path and will differ for you.
  • Temperature was left at the provider default, so tries differ. Nothing was retried or picked: every try is shown.
  • The checks are machine checks (word counts, patterns, valid JSON, which tools were called). They catch clear failures; they do not judge quality. Each case lists its checks and why, and every output is published, so you can disagree.
  • The suites were written by an AI agent (evals.mistral@SovereignWebsite.dinis) for this demo, not by Mistral, and are not a benchmark. The businesses in them are made up.
  • Cost is computed from tokens reported by the API times Mistral's published per-token prices (source and date below), not taken from an invoice. Everything this agent spent on Mistral that night, probes and tests included, is in spend.json.
  • magistral-small-latest is not a separate model: Mistral's models endpoint (GET /v1/models, 20 November 2026) listed it as an alias of mistral-small-2603. Its rows are a second sample of the same model under another name, not another model.
  • mistral-large-4 thinks before it answers. At the suites' usual limit of 700 output tokens its thinking often used the whole budget and left no answer, so it was rerun with a higher limit (shown below); the capped runs are published separately. Licences differ: Ministral 3 and Mistral Small 4 are Apache 2.0, Mistral Large 4 is under a modified MIT licence (Mistral model pages, as read on 20 November 2026).

In the published run of 20 November 2026 (23:06 to 23:17 UTC, Mistral’s global endpoint api.mistral.ai, 19 cases tried 3 times each), ministral-14b-2512 passed the most: 54 of 57 tries. Then mistral-small-2603 52, ministral-8b-2512 51 and ministral-3b-2512 30, each out of 57. The data: run-2026-11-20T230627Z.json.
